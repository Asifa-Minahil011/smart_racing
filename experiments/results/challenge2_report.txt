PART 3. Limitation of one weighted scalar cost

Pareto analysis, leg 3 -> 4: 6 candidate simple paths (<= 8 roads), objectives (time, fuel, risk)
  Pareto-optimal routes: 3
  chosen by at least one of 3000 random weight vectors: 3
  Every Pareto-optimal route on this leg is chosen by some weights, so this circuit shows no such gap; the constructed example below does.

Constructed example: three parallel roads, weights only on time and risk
  A fast+dangerous   normalised time 0.20  risk 1.00
  B compromise       normalised time 0.72  risk 0.50
  C slow+safe        normalised time 1.00  risk 0.00
  A fast+dangerous   chosen for w_time in [0.56, 1.00]
  B compromise       chosen for NO weight at all (101 values tried)
  C slow+safe        chosen for w_time in [0.00, 0.55]
  B is not dominated (A is faster, C is safer) yet no weighted sum ever picks it:
  it lies above the straight line joining A and C in (time, risk) space.
